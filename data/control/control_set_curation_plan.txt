Electride Control-Set Curation Plan

The control set should contain structurally and chemically similar materials rather than random inorganic structures. These materials should initially be treated as control candidates or unclassified candidates, not confirmed non-electrides, because some may show interstitial-electron behavior after electronic calculations.

For each electride or proposed electride phase, search for:

- The same composition in different polymorphs.
- Materials with the same structural prototype but different elements.
- Chemically nearby elemental substitutions.
- Structures with similar crystal geometry and candidate-void environments.
- Chemically related compounds with different structures.
- Large-cavity, open-framework, layered, or channel structures that may be geometric false positives.
- Materials with ordinary bond or lone-pair localization that could be confused with electride behavior.

Use Materials Project structures and identifiers where available, together with pymatgen structure matching, crystal-structure fingerprints, prototype matching, composition fingerprints, and local-environment or void descriptors. RDKit is not the preferred tool because it is designed primarily for molecular structures rather than inorganic crystals.

The selection process should be:

1. Start with each electride seed phase.
2. Find same-formula polymorphs.
3. Find same-prototype structures with different chemistry.
4. Find chemically similar substitutions.
5. Find structures with similar local void geometry.
6. Remove known electrides and already identified candidates.
7. Remove duplicate structure-condition records.
8. Retain the remaining materials as unclassified control candidates.

Do not select only the closest structural matches. Combine very close controls with broader chemical, structural, and void-geometry controls. Use quotas to prevent one electride family or prototype from dominating the dataset. A preliminary target of approximately three to five control phases per electride phase is reasonable, subject to availability and diversity.

Pressure must be treated explicitly. High-pressure electrides should, where possible, be compared with related structures at similar pressures or with alternative phases of the same composition. Ambient structures should not automatically be treated as appropriate controls for high-pressure phases.

Before electronic calculations, use provisional categories such as:

- known_electride
- electride_candidate
- control_candidate
- unclassified

After electronic calculations, assign the physical outcome using ELF, interstitial charge, electron density, and related descriptors:

- strong interstitial-electron state
- weak or electride-like state
- non-electride
- ambiguous

The final control set should therefore be a deliberately difficult comparison set designed to prevent the model from learning shortcuts such as “large void,” “electropositive elements,” or “metallic structure” equals electride.
