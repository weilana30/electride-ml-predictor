Conduct a comprehensive, source-verified literature search for inorganic crystalline electrides and proposed inorganic electride candidates.

The goal is to create a master registry of every distinct inorganic crystalline phase that has been:

1. experimentally confirmed as an electride;
2. experimentally suggested or partially characterized as an electride;
3. theoretically predicted to be an electride;
4. described as electride-like or a potential electride with credible interstitial-electron evidence.

Do not use or rely on any previously curated electride dataset. Build the registry independently from the scientific literature and primary sources.

## Definition

Include a material only if the paper explicitly identifies it as an electride, electride candidate, or electride-like material, or provides credible evidence that electrons occupy interstitial, non-nuclear regions of a periodic inorganic crystal.

Acceptable evidence includes:

- electron localization function maxima in interstitial regions;
- interstitial electron density;
- non-nuclear attractors;
- interstitial Bader or topological basins;
- integrated electron population in a crystal cavity;
- electronic bands identified as interstitial-electron states;
- charge-density or ELF connectivity through interstitial regions;
- experimental observations explicitly interpreted as electride behavior.

Do not classify a material as an electride merely because it is:

- metallic;
- electron-rich;
- composed of electropositive elements;
- vacancy-containing;
- predicted to have a large cavity;
- associated with an unusual band structure;
- associated with ordinary covalent bonds or lone pairs.

## Scope

Include:

- inorganic bulk crystalline materials;
- elemental inorganic electrides;
- binary, ternary, and multinary inorganic compounds;
- ambient-pressure phases;
- high-pressure phases;
- pressure-induced phase transitions;
- defect- or vacancy-induced inorganic electrides;
- doped or substituted inorganic electride phases;
- layered, 0D, 1D, 2D, and 3D inorganic electrides;
- experimentally known structures and theoretically predicted structures;
- distinct polymorphs and pressure-dependent phases.

Exclude from the main registry:

- organic electrides;
- molecular electrides;
- solvated-electron systems;
- metal–ammonia electrides;
- isolated clusters without a periodic crystalline phase;
- surfaces and interfaces unless they are part of a clearly defined periodic inorganic crystal;
- materials with no explicit electride claim or credible interstitial-electron evidence.

If an excluded material is important historically, record it in a separate excluded-materials section with the reason for exclusion.

## Search strategy

Use multiple research passes rather than one search.

### Pass 1: Reviews and foundational literature

Find major reviews and perspective articles on:

- inorganic electrides;
- theoretical electride descriptors;
- high-pressure electrides;
- layered and two-dimensional electrides;
- interstitial or anionic electrons;
- non-nuclear attractors;
- high-throughput electride screening.

Use these papers to identify terminology, material families, and references to individual compounds.

### Pass 2: Experimentally confirmed electrides

Search for every experimentally synthesized or characterized inorganic electride. For each material, locate the original experimental paper whenever possible.

Do not treat a review statement as sufficient confirmation if the original paper can be identified.

### Pass 3: Theoretically predicted electrides

Search for:

- first-principles predictions;
- high-throughput electride screening;
- crystal-structure prediction;
- evolutionary structure searches;
- inverse-design studies;
- high-pressure structure searches;
- materials-discovery studies;
- papers containing “potential electride,” “predicted electride,” “electride candidate,” “interstitial electron,” “anionic electron,” or “electride-like.”

Inspect tables, figures, appendices, and supplementary information. Candidate formulas may appear only in supplementary files.

### Pass 4: Family-specific searches

Search separately by material family, including:

- alkali and alkaline-earth compounds;
- alkaline-earth nitrides;
- metal oxides and chalcogenides;
- intermetallic electrides;
- rare-earth and transition-metal systems;
- layered electrides;
- high-pressure elemental phases;
- hydrides and superhydrides;
- vacancy and defect electrides;
- electron-rich intermetallic compounds.

Search both formula-based and concept-based terminology.

### Pass 5: Citation expansion

For each major review, screening paper, and foundational electride paper:

- inspect cited references;
- inspect citing papers;
- inspect supplementary information;
- search every newly discovered material formula;
- search alternative formula orderings and material names;
- search the DOI, title, formula, and prototype independently.

Continue until repeated searches produce mostly duplicates and no substantial new material families.

## Required verification for every entry

Before including a material in the verified registry, confirm:

1. The formula appears in an identifiable source.
2. The material is inorganic.
3. The phase or structure is distinguishable from other phases of the same composition.
4. The electride claim or interstitial-electron evidence is present.
5. Experimental and theoretical status are correctly distinguished.
6. Pressure is recorded when relevant.
7. Defects, vacancies, substitutions, or doping are recorded when required.
8. The crystal structure and space group are not invented.
9. The dimensionality is supported by the source or marked unclear.
10. A DOI or stable source link is provided whenever available.

If a detail cannot be verified, write `not reported` rather than inferring it.

## Status categories

Use exactly one primary status:

- `experimentally_confirmed`
- `experimentally_suggested`
- `theoretically_predicted`
- `ambiguous_or_disputed`

Use a separate confidence field:

- `high`
- `medium`
- `low`

Do not upgrade theoretical predictions to experimental confirmations.

## Dimensionality categories

Use:

- `0D`
- `1D`
- `2D`
- `3D`
- `mixed`
- `unclear`

If different papers assign different dimensionalities, preserve the disagreement in the notes and use `mixed` or `unclear` as appropriate.

## Deduplication rules

Create one row per distinct inorganic material phase, not one row per paper.

Keep separate rows when the same composition has different:

- crystal structures;
- space groups;
- polymorphs;
- pressure ranges;
- defect configurations;
- dopant concentrations;
- electride dimensionalities;
- structural prototypes.

Merge rows only when they clearly refer to the same phase.

Normalize formulas while preserving the original reported formula. Do not merge different vacancy, substitutional, pressure, or polymorphic phases merely because their reduced formulas are similar.

## Required master-table columns

Return a table with exactly these columns:

1. `material_id`
2. `normalized_formula`
3. `formula_as_reported`
4. `material_name`
5. `electride_status`
6. `confidence`
7. `electride_dimensionality`
8. `material_category`
9. `crystal_structure_or_prototype`
10. `space_group`
11. `pressure`
12. `temperature_or_synthesis_conditions`
13. `defect_dopant_or_vacancy_details`
14. `reported_evidence`
15. `evidence_type`
16. `primary_reference`
17. `publication_year`
18. `doi_or_stable_link`
19. `additional_references`
20. `structure_source_or_database_id`
21. `verification_notes`

Use `not reported` when necessary. Never invent missing information.

## Evidence recording

For `reported_evidence`, provide a concise paraphrase of the specific evidence supporting the electride classification.

For `evidence_type`, use one or more of:

- `ELF`
- `interstitial_charge_density`
- `non_nuclear_attractor`
- `interstitial_bader_or_topological_basin`
- `interstitial_electronic_bands`
- `density_connectivity`
- `experimental_interpretation`
- `multiple_electronic_descriptors`

Record whether the evidence comes from the primary paper, supplementary information, or a later paper.

## Quality-control summary

After the master table, provide:

### Count summary

Report:

- total distinct inorganic phases;
- experimentally confirmed count;
- experimentally suggested count;
- theoretically predicted count;
- ambiguous or disputed count;
- counts by 0D, 1D, 2D, 3D, mixed, and unclear;
- counts by ambient-pressure, high-pressure, defect/vacancy, and other categories.

### Potential duplicates or ambiguous cases

List:

- formulas that may refer to the same phase;
- polymorphs requiring separate treatment;
- conflicting dimensionality assignments;
- materials whose electride classification is disputed;
- records where the original source could not be accessed.

### Likely gaps

Identify:

- inaccessible papers;
- unavailable supplementary information;
- candidate lists found only in secondary sources;
- material families that require additional searches;
- formulas for which a crystal structure could not be located.

### Search log

Briefly record:

- databases and websites searched;
- major search terms used;
- reviews and screening papers used as citation hubs;
- the date of the search;
- the stopping criterion;
- searches that produced substantial new entries.

Do not claim that the list is literally complete. Describe it as comprehensive relative to the accessible literature and clearly identify remaining gaps.

Before presenting the final table, perform a second verification pass over every row for formula accuracy, source accuracy, inorganic-only scope, electride status, phase identity, pressure, and duplicate handling.

If file creation is available, also export the verified master table as both CSV and XLSX. The files must contain only the verified registry and use the exact column names above.
